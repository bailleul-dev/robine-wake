The Zero W build is a Pi Zero W, the InnoMaker amplifier HAT, two speakers and
the ESP32 that drives the light strip. The Wi-Fi bedside button needs no wiring
to the Pi.

:::danger[Before you start]
- Wire with everything **unplugged**; the supply goes last.
- Speakers are **bridged (BTL)**: the "−" of a speaker is never tied to ground.
- The HAT outputs **5 V on the header** and normally powers the Pi through it.
  Do **not** also power the Pi from its `PWR` micro-USB port.
- The pin assignment below comes from the Linux `merus-amp` overlay for Infineon
  MA12 amplifiers and has to be confirmed on your board: do the **continuity
  check** of section 2 before connecting the ESP32.
:::

## The 40-pin header at a glance

The HAT sits on the Zero's header (same 40 pins and numbering as the Pi 3).
Pin numbers are *physical* pin numbers. Odd pins on the left (inner row), even
pins on the right (outer row, nearest the board edge); pin 1 is the square pad.

## Finding the pins when the HAT hides the names

With the amplifier fitted, the names printed on the Pi are covered. Find a pin
by **position**, not by name:

1. Turn the board so the **SD card slot is on your left** and the 40-pin header
   runs along the **top edge**.
2. The header has two rows of 20 pins. The **top row** (nearest the board edge)
   is the **even** pins `2, 4, 6 … 40`; the **bottom row** is the **odd** pins
   `1, 3, 5 … 39`.
3. Count from the **left** end: pin 1 is the first of the bottom row, pin 2 the
   first of the top row. Pin `n` is the **⌈n ÷ 2⌉-th** pin of its row.

| Pin | Row | Count from the left | Function here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | top | 1st | 5 V → ESP32 `VIN` |
| 6 | top | 3rd | GND → ESP32 |
| 8 | top | 4th | TXD → ESP32 `D18` |
| 10 | top | 5th | RXD ← ESP32 `TX2` |

**Check you are on the right pin** with a continuity tester, everything
unpowered: pin 6 must beep with pins 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34 and 39 (all ground), and
pin 2 must beep with pin 4 (both 5 V) and with nothing else. If not, you are one
position off, or on the wrong row: pin 1 right next to pin 2 is 3.3 V, and
wiring that to `VIN` would starve the ESP32.

The other highlighted pins belong to the HAT (I²C, I²S): no wire of yours goes
there.

## 1. Audio — InnoMaker HiFi AMP Pro Mini (MA12070P)

**You need:** Pi Zero W, the InnoMaker HAT, a **regulated 19–20 V DC supply,
4 A or more**, with a 5.5 mm jack (see *Power tolerances* below), two passive
4–8 Ω speakers.

1. Fit the HAT on the Zero's header, all 40 pins seated (solder the header on
   the Zero first if it has none).
2. Connect both speakers, respecting polarity. Do not join the two "−" wires.
3. Leave the Zero's `PWR` micro-USB **empty**.
4. Connect the supply last, then switch it on. The HAT is expected to feed the
   Zero with its 5 V (as the bench notes found); if the Zero stays dark, switch
   off and check that the header is fully seated before trying anything else.

### Power tolerances

| What | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Amplifier chip (MA12070P) supply, recommended | **4 – 26 V** | Infineon datasheet, table 6-1 |
| Amplifier chip, absolute maximum | **27.5 V** (damage above) | datasheet, table 5-1 |
| HAT DC input (5.5 mm jack **or** green terminal) | **9 – 24 V** | InnoMaker AMP Pro page (the Mini has the same power inputs) |
| Jack and green terminal together | **forbidden** — one source only | InnoMaker |
| Speaker load | **4 – 8 Ω** per channel; never below 3.2 Ω | datasheet table 6-1 (bridged output) |
| Supply power to provide | 1.1 – 1.3 × the combined speaker power | InnoMaker |
| Ratings | 2 × 30 W continuous (8 Ω, 22 V) · 2 × 80 W peak (4 Ω, 26 V) | InnoMaker / Infineon |
| Hot-plugging the supply | **not allowed** (inrush, sparks) | InnoMaker |

What it means for this build:

- **Use a regulated 19 or 20 V adapter** (a laptop-style brick). That sits inside
  the HAT's 9–24 V window and the chip's 26 V limit with margin: even +5 % is
  21 V.
- **Do not use a "24 V" adapter.** InnoMaker warns that some surge to 30 V or more at
  switch-on, above the chip's 27.5 V absolute maximum. Avoid unregulated
  supplies for the same reason.
- **Current:** for the full 2 × 30 W, 1.3 × 60 W ≈ 78 W, so 20 V and about 4 A.
  InnoMaker's example is 20 V / 5–6 A. A 3 A supply is enough only if the
  volume stays moderate; the project's rule is 25 % margin over the *measured*
  peak, so measure it first (3 A is fine if the peak is under 2.4 A).
- **Never put two speakers in parallel on one channel** unless the result stays
  at or above 4 Ω: two 8 Ω speakers in parallel make 4 Ω, two 4 Ω make 2 Ω, which
  is below the chip's limit.
- **Only one 5 V source for the Zero.** The HAT powers it through the header
  (InnoMaker: *"the HAT will provide power to the Raspberry Pi"*) and warns
  that extra 5 V into the Pi may damage it. So: nothing in the `PWR` micro-USB,
  and a computer cable on the data port must be data-only.
- **Order:** plug the supply into the HAT first, and only then switch the
  adapter on. Never hot-plug the DC jack.
- **Unknown:** the current the HAT's 5 V rail can deliver to the Pi is not
  documented. A Zero W needs roughly 0.1 – 0.35 A; if it reboots while the amp
  plays loud, measure the 5 V at header pin 2 under load.
- **Speaker terminals (Mini):** InnoMaker states the *right* speaker is on
  `PIN-3` (+) and `PIN-4` (−) of the speaker connector; the left speaker is
  then presumably on `PIN-1` and `PIN-2` (not stated). Read the silkscreen: the Mini is wired differently from the full-size
  AMP Pro.

Sources: [Infineon MA12070 datasheet](https://cdn.sparkfun.com/assets/8/6/d/d/4/MA12070_Datasheet.pdf)
(V1.0, 2018-07-17) and [InnoMaker AMP-Pro](https://github.com/INNO-MAKER/AMP-Pro).

## 2. Light — ESP32 and LED strip

The ESP32 and the strip are wired exactly as on the Pi 3 (see
[Wiring — current setup](../wiring-pi3/#2-light--esp32-and-led-strip)). Two
things differ on the Zero: the UART pins are shared with the amplifier HAT, and
the Zero has no free USB port, so the ESP32 takes its 5 V from the header.

:::danger[Continuity check first — Pi pins 8 and 10]
The stock amplifier overlay uses GPIO14 (pin 8) as *enable* and GPIO15 (pin 10)
as *mute*. The `rpi0` firmware loads `merus-amp-uart`, which does **not** use
them, so the UART can. That only works if the HAT does not depend on those two
pins.

With **everything unpowered**, take the HAT alone and test with a continuity
tester whether its connector pins 8 and 10 go to the amplifier chip (enable,
mute) or nowhere.

- **Nowhere** → go ahead: the HAT stays enabled by its own resistors.
- **They go to the chip** → do not wire the UART on these pins: the ESP32's idle
  UART line (high) would hold the amplifier muted or disabled. Bend out or cut
  pins 8 and 10 on the HAT's connector so they do not touch the Zero, and tell
  me: the HAT then needs the stock `merus-amp` overlay, so the ESP32 needs
  another link (Wi-Fi).
:::

1. Pass the continuity check above.
2. Join all grounds: Pi pin 6, ESP32 GND, strip GND, 5 V supply GND.
3. Wire the UART: Pi pin 8 → ESP32 `D18`, Pi pin 10 ← ESP32 `TX2`.
4. 330 Ω in series on the data line, close to the strip's `DIN`; the strip's
   `+5V`/`GND` come from the 5 V supply, not from the ESP32 or the Pi.
5. Power the ESP32 from the HAT's 5 V: header pin 2 (or 4) → ESP32 `VIN`.
   `pi_light` runs without Wi-Fi, so it draws about 50–80 mA; the strip has its
   own supply. The HAT's 5 V budget is not documented, so measure pin 2 under
   load if the Zero resets.
6. Flash the ESP32 from your computer over its USB-C cable; on the Pi 3 it is
   flashed through the Pi, which is not set up on the Zero. **Unplug the `VIN`
   wire first**, and never leave USB-C and `VIN` powered at the same time: many
   DevKits have no diode between them and the 5 V can back-feed.
7. From the web interface, start a sunrise or the reading light.

:::note
`guide_light` is enabled on the `rpi0` target with the same UART device
(`ttyS0`) as the Pi 3.
:::

## 3. Bedside button

The Wi-Fi button calls the clock over Wi-Fi: **no wire goes to the Zero**, so it
does not conflict with the HAT. See [Wiring — Wi-Fi bedside button](../wiring-remote-button/).
The LoRa button receiver (SPI on pins 19/21/23/24) is not available on the
Zero with this HAT: chip select GPIO8 is claimed by the amplifier overlay.

one nearer the middle of the board, labelled `USB`) into an Ethernet gadget

## First power-up checks (audio)

1. **Unpowered**, with a continuity tester: pin 16 goes to the amplifier chip's
   *error* output and pin 11 to its booster control (pins 8 and 10: see the
   continuity check in section 2). If not, the overlay does not match your board.
2. Power the HAT **alone** (not on the Zero) and measure the header: pin 1
   should be 3.3 V or nothing, SDA and SCL (pins 3 and 5) 0 V or 3.3 V — **never 5 V**.
3. Fit it on the Zero, power up at **low volume**, and play a manual test
   sound from the web interface.
4. The firmware's volume curve was tuned on the HiFiBerry; the MA12070P master
   volume starts from −144 dB. Raise the level in small steps.

If the card does not appear (no sound, `snd_rpi_merus_amp` absent), the HAT
does not answer on I²C address `0x20`, or the overlay does not fit it.

## Reference table

| Function | Pin | GPIO | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| I²C data / clock | 3 / 5 | 2 / 3 | amplifier control, address `0x20` |
| I²S clock | 12 | 18 | bit clock |
| I²S frame | 35 | 19 | left/right clock |
| I²S data out | 40 | 21 | audio to the amplifier |
| ESP32 UART TXD / RXD | 8 / 10 | 14 / 15 | `ttyS0`; not the amplifier enable/mute (overlay `merus-amp-uart`) |
| Booster | 11 | 17 | overlay `booster_gp` |
| Amplifier error | 16 | 23 | overlay `error_gp` |
| 5 V out of the HAT | 2, 4 | — | powers the Zero |

The audio chain is limited to 48 kHz by the Linux driver: music and alarms play
normally at 44.1 or 48 kHz.
