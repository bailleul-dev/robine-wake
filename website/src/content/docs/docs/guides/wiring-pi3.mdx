## Your current setup

| Part | What it is |
|---|---|
| Computer | Raspberry Pi 3 B/B+ running the Nerves firmware |
| Audio | HiFiBerry Amp2 **stacked on the Pi's 40-pin header**, two speakers |
| Light controller | **One ESP32 DevKit, 30-pin** (classic ESP32 with a CP2102 USB-C chip — *not* an ESP32-S3). It is powered from the Pi's 5 V pin (header pin 2 or 4 → ESP32 `VIN`) and talks to the Pi over its UART pins (GPIO14/15), both reached under the Amp2 |
| Light | 60-LED WS2812B strip on the ESP32's `D13`, with its own 5 V supply |
| Bedside button | optional: LoRa module on the Pi's SPI |

There is **no second ESP32** and no ESP32-S3 in this build. (The two-ESP32
version is the [future board bench](../wiring-bench/).)

Three independent circuits hang on the Pi 3. Build them one at a time and
check each before the next: **audio**, **light**, **bedside button**.

:::danger[Before you start]
- Wire with everything **unplugged**. Connect power last.
- Speakers are **bridged (BTL)**: the "−" of a speaker is never tied to ground.
- Never put 5 V on a 3.3 V pin (LoRa module, GPIO). Never transmit on the LoRa
  module without its antenna.
:::

## The 40-pin header at a glance

Pin numbers are *physical* pin numbers (not GPIO numbers). The chart is drawn as
on a pinout poster: pin 1 at the top, **odd pins on the left** (the inner row)
and **even pins on the right** (the outer row, nearest the edge of the board).
Pin 1 is the square pad, next to the corner of the board closest to the SD slot.

## Finding the pins when the HAT hides the names

With the amplifier fitted, the names printed on the Pi are covered. Find a pin
by **position**, not by name:

1. Turn the board so the **SD card slot is on your left** and the 40-pin header
   runs along the **top edge**.
2. The header has two rows of 20 pins. The **top row** (nearest the board edge)
   is the **even** pins `2, 4, 6 … 40`; the **bottom row** is the **odd** pins
   `1, 3, 5 … 39`.
3. Count from the **left** end: pin 1 is the first of the bottom row, pin 2 the
   first of the top row. Pin `n` is the **⌈n ÷ 2⌉-th** pin of its row.

| Pin | Row | Count from the left | Function here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | top | 3rd | GND → ESP32 |
| 8 | top | 4th | TXD → ESP32 `D18` |
| 10 | top | 5th | RXD ← ESP32 `TX2` |
| 17 | bottom | 9th | 3V3 → LoRa |
| 19 | bottom | 10th | MOSI → LoRa |
| 21 | bottom | 11th | MISO ← LoRa |
| 23 | bottom | 12th | SCLK → LoRa |
| 24 | top | 12th | CE0 → LoRa NSS |
| 25 | bottom | 13th | GND → LoRa |

**Check you are on the right pin** with a continuity tester, everything
unpowered: pin 6 must beep with pins 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34 and 39 (all ground).
If it does not, you are one position off, or on the wrong row.

The Amp2 itself needs no wire: it takes the I²C and I²S pins and feeds the Pi
with 5 V simply by sitting on the header.

1. Fit the Amp2 on the Pi's 40-pin header, all 40 pins seated.
2. Connect both speakers, respecting polarity. Do not join the two "−" wires.

4. Boot, open the web interface and play a manual test sound at low volume.
   The card is detected automatically (`alsa_device: "auto"`).

## 2. Light — ESP32 and LED strip

**You need:** ESP32 DevKit 30-pin (CP2102), a 60-LED WS2812B strip, a
separate **5 V** supply sized for the strip (up to 3.6 A at full white; the
firmware caps the brightness), a 330 Ω resistor, Dupont wires.

Prefer a soldered board? The [Pi 3 HAT perfboard](../wiring-pi3-hat/) carries the ESP32, the diode, the resistor, the strip terminal and the capacitor on top of the Amp2.

1. Join the **grounds**: Pi GND, ESP32 GND, strip GND and the 5 V supply GND
   must all meet. Without a common ground the data signal is garbled.
2. Wire the UART: Pi pin 8 → ESP32 `D18`, Pi pin 10 ← ESP32 `TX2`.
3. Put the 330 Ω resistor in series on the data line, close to the strip's
   `DIN` (the end where the arrows point *into* the strip).
4. Feed the strip's `+5V`/`GND` from the 5 V supply, not from the ESP32 or the
   Pi.
5. Power the ESP32 from the Pi: header pin 2 (or 4, both are 5 V) → ESP32
   `VIN`. About 50–80 mA, well within what the Amp2 supplies to the Pi. Do
   **not** also plug a USB cable into the ESP32 while `VIN` is wired: two 5 V
   sources feed into each other. To flash the ESP32 from the Pi's USB port,
   unplug the `VIN` wire first, flash, then put it back and unplug the USB cable.
6. Check from the web interface: on the evening screen (*Soir*), start a
   sunrise or the reading light.

:::tip[Optional, good practice]
A 1000 µF capacitor across the strip's `+5V`/`GND` at the supply end protects
the LEDs from the switch-on surge.
:::

## 3. Bedside button — LoRa module (SX1276 / RFM95)

**You need:** an RFM95 (or Ra-01H) 868 MHz module, a 868 MHz antenna (a plain
wire of about 86 mm works), a 10 kΩ resistor, 100 nF and 10 µF capacitors.

Prefer a soldered board? The [Pi 3 HAT perfboard](../wiring-pi3-hat/#step-4--lora-connector) brings the six Pi wires of this section to a
6-pin header, **J5**. The module then joins J5 by a short cable, with the same names:

| J5 pin | Module pin | Pi header pin |
|---|---|---|
| 3V3 | 3.3V | 17 |
| MOSI | MOSI | 19 |
| MISO | MISO | 21 |
| NSS | NSS (CS) | 24 (CE0) |
| SCLK | SCK | 23 |
| GND | GND | 25 |

`RESET`, `DIO0` and the two decoupling capacitors stay on the module, as in the figure below.

On power-up the driver reads the module's version register and expects `0x12`.
Any other value (loose wire, wrong module) puts the radio in an *unavailable*
state instead of listening with a broken radio. A constant `0` (`unexpected_sx1276_version, 0` in the log)
means the module does not answer at all: it is not powered or MISO/NSS is not connected. Protocol and pairing:
[remote button note](../../notes/remote-alarm-button/).

## Power-up order

1. All grounds joined, all wires checked against the figures.
2. Speakers connected.
3. Antenna on the LoRa module.
4. ESP32 `VIN` wire to the Pi's 5 V (it is only live once the Amp2 supply is on).
5. Amp2 supply (this powers the Pi), then the 5 V strip supply.
6. Never hot-plug the Amp2 supply.
