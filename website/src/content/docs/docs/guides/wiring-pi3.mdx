Three independent circuits hang on the Pi 3. Build them one at a time and
check each before the next: **audio**, **light**, **bedside button**.

:::danger[Before you start]
- Wire with everything **unplugged**. Connect power last.
- Speakers are **bridged (BTL)**: the "−" of a speaker is never tied to ground.
- Never put 5 V on a 3.3 V pin (LoRa module, GPIO). Never transmit on the LoRa
  module without its antenna.
:::

## The 40-pin header at a glance

Pin numbers are *physical* pin numbers (not GPIO numbers). The chart is drawn as
on a pinout poster: pin 1 at the top, **odd pins on the left** (the inner row)
and **even pins on the right** (the outer row, nearest the edge of the board).
Pin 1 is the square pad, next to the corner of the board closest to the SD slot.

**You need:** Pi 3 B/B+, HiFiBerry Amp2, a regulated **16.5 V / 2 A** supply,
two passive 4–8 Ω speakers.

1. Fit the Amp2 on the Pi's 40-pin header, all 40 pins seated.
2. Connect both speakers, respecting polarity. Do not join the two "−" wires.
3. Connect the supply **last**, then switch it on.
4. Boot, open the web interface and play a manual test sound at low volume.
   The card is detected automatically (`alsa_device: "auto"`).

## 2. Light — ESP32 and LED strip

**You need:** ESP32 DevKit 30-pin (CP2102, USB), a 60-LED WS2812B strip, a
separate **5 V** supply sized for the strip (up to 3.6 A at full white; the
firmware caps the brightness), a 330 Ω resistor, Dupont wires.

1. Join the **grounds**: Pi GND, ESP32 GND, strip GND and the 5 V supply GND
   must all meet. Without a common ground the data signal is garbled.
2. Wire the UART: Pi pin 8 → ESP32 `D18`, Pi pin 10 ← ESP32 `TX2`.
3. Put the 330 Ω resistor in series on the data line, close to the strip's
   `DIN` (the end where the arrows point *into* the strip).
4. Feed the strip's `+5V`/`GND` from the 5 V supply, not from the ESP32 or the
   Pi.
5. Plug the ESP32 into a Pi USB port (power, and flashing).
6. Check from the web interface: on the evening screen (*Soir*), start a
   sunrise or the reading light.

:::tip[Optional, good practice]
A 1000 µF capacitor across the strip's `+5V`/`GND` at the supply end protects
the LEDs from the switch-on surge.
:::

## 3. Bedside button — LoRa module (SX1276 / RFM95)

**You need:** an RFM95 (or Ra-01H) 868 MHz module, a 868 MHz antenna (a plain
wire of about 86 mm works), a 10 kΩ resistor, 100 nF and 10 µF capacitors.

On power-up the driver reads the module's version register and expects `0x12`.
Any other value (loose wire, wrong module) puts the radio in an *unavailable*
state instead of listening with a broken radio. Protocol and pairing:
[remote button note](../../notes/remote-alarm-button/).

## Power-up order

1. All grounds joined, all wires checked against the figures.
2. Speakers connected.
3. Antenna on the LoRa module.
4. ESP32 USB cable.
5. Amp2 supply (this powers the Pi), then the 5 V strip supply.
6. Never hot-plug the Amp2 supply.

## Reference table

| From | Pin | To | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pi | 8 · GPIO14 TXD | ESP32 `D18` | UART, mini UART `ttyS0` |
| Pi | 10 · GPIO15 RXD | ESP32 `TX2` | UART |
| Pi | 6 · GND | ESP32 GND | common ground |
| ESP32 | `D13` | strip `DIN` | via 330 Ω |
| 5 V supply | `+5 V` / GND | strip `+5V` / GND | joined to ESP32 GND |
| Pi | 17 · 3V3 | LoRa 3.3V | + 100 nF, 10 µF |
| Pi | 25 · GND | LoRa GND | any GND pin works |
| Pi | 23 · SCLK | LoRa SCK | SPI0 |
| Pi | 21 · MISO | LoRa MISO | SPI0 |
| Pi | 19 · MOSI | LoRa MOSI | SPI0 |
| Pi | 24 · CE0 | LoRa NSS | SPI0 chip select |
| LoRa | RESET | 3.3 V via 10 kΩ | pull-up |
